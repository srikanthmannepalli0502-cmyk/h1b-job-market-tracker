How to read this

  • An LCA is not a visa. Employers file a Labor Condition Application before an H-1B petition. A certified LCA shows intent to sponsor for that role and wage; it does not mean a visa was approved.
  • Salaries are offered wages (the lower end of the range on the application), converted to a yearly amount. Values outside $15K–$1M are treated as data-entry errors and excluded from salary figures.
  • The same job goes by different titles. Amazon, for example, files its analyst roles as “Business Intelligence Engineer”, so it appears under BI Analyst / Engineer, not Data Analyst. Check neighboring roles when you search for sponsors.
  • Roles are classified from job titles with transparent rules (see the rules), so some titles will be missed or misclassified.
  • Employer names are grouped by normalizing spelling and legal suffixes. Separate legal entities (e.g. Amazon.com Services and Amazon Web Services) are listed separately.
  • Partial fiscal years are labeled. Year-over-year changes always compare the same months.

Source: U.S. Department of Labor, Office of Foreign Labor Certification, LCA disclosure data. Personal details in the source files (contact, attorney and preparer names, emails, phones) are dropped during ingestion. Not legal advice. Code: GitHub. Built by Srikanth Mannepalli.
